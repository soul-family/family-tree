# General

## Description

A family tree, also called a genealogical tree or a pedigree chart, is a chart representing family relationships in a conventional tree structure. More detailed family trees, used in medicine and social work, are known as genograms.

## Overview

Common family tree chart types include:

- **Pedigree Chart / Ancestor Chart** — shows three to five generations of ancestors in tree form; uses Ahnentafel numbering to assign each person a unique number
- **Descendant Tree** — starts with one person and branches downward through children and grandchildren; available in standard and fan chart formats
- **Ancestor Tree** — shows a person’s ancestors in standard, fan, or vertical layouts
- **Hourglass Tree** — places the starting individual in the middle with ancestors above and descendants below
- **Fan Chart** — circular or semicircular layout with the starting individual in the center and generations radiating outward
- **All-in-One Tree** — shows ancestors, descendants, and cousins simultaneously; useful for overview but quickly becomes cluttered
- **Outline Descendant Tree** — compact indented list of descendants without boxes; ideal for family history books
- **Family Group Record** — summarizes a couple and their children with vital-record details
- **Register Report** — narrative descendant report using Roman numerals for children and Arabic numerals for parents
- **NGS Quarterly Report** — modified Register report using dual numbering for descendants
- **Genealogy Report** — narrative history of ancestors or descendants in book form
- **Ahnentafel Numbering** — genealogical numbering system where doubling a person’s number gives the father’s number, adding one gives the mother’s number
- **Timeline Chart** — horizontal bars showing life spans so you can see who was alive in a given year
- **Life Bar Chart** — timeline with historical events alongside individual milestones
- **Kinship Report** — alphabetical list of names with each person’s relationship to a selected individual
- **Cascading Pedigree Charts** — automated series of numbered pedigree charts for printing
- **Tiny Tafel Report** — surname summary with Soundex codes, birth-year ranges, and locations
- **Waterfall Chart** — descendant chart cascading diagonally from upper left to lower right

## Why

- Tree structure makes lineage easy to read at a glance.

- Supports both biological and social relationships depending on the notation.

- Works as a bridge between archival records and modern visualization tools.

- Standardized formats like GEDCOM allow data to move between tools without loss.

## Syntax

No single standard syntax. Different tools use different notations:

- GEDCOM for data exchange
- SchemaTex for text-based pedigree diagrams
- Mermaid.js for flowchart-based trees
- FamilyTreeJS for interactive JSON-driven trees

Choose the format that matches the tool you are exporting to or rendering from.
